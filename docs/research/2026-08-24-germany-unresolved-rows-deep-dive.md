## saarland

Open question: is Saarland University Master's admission rolling/continuous, or does an application portal open/close on a schedule?

All four extra angles were tried. (1) **German-language page**: fetched `https://www.uni-saarland.de/studium/angebot/master/informatik.html` (the correct German URL, found via the page's own `hreflang="de"` alternate link after the first guessed URL 404'd) — its "Bewerbung" section is word-for-word equivalent to the English page: "Die Bewerbung erfolgt über das Online-Bewerbungsportal der Saarbrücker Informatik" (application is via the online portal of Saarbrücken Informatics), with no separate portal-opening date. (2) **Department-specific Informatik page**: the program table explicitly states "Restricted entry: No" and lists only the same early/late deadline pairs (winter: early 15 Nov / late 15 May; summer: early 15 May / late 15 Nov) — no opening date. (3) **FAQ/Bewerbungsverfahren angle**: checked the university-wide "Applying for a Master's programme" page (`uni-saarland.de/en/study/application/master.html`) and the "International applicants" page. These reveal that Saarland runs **two separate application systems**: the central "SIM portals" (for programmes centrally administered) explicitly state "The SIM application portals are only accessible during the application period" — i.e. SIM genuinely has an open/close window — but the same page says Computer Science is one of the programmes where "the application and admission procedures are dealt with by the relevant departments," meaning CS uses its own department portal (formerly `oas.cs.uni-saarland.de`, now migrated to `apply.cs.uni-saarland.de` — the old OAS closed 28 Feb 2026 per a fresh web search), not SIM. No page anywhere states an opening date for the department's own portal. (4) **Wayback Machine**: checked CDX snapshots of the English informatics programme page from Jan 2025 through Aug 2026 (11 captures) and fetched the 14 Aug 2026 snapshot in full — identical deadline-only language across the whole period, no portal-opening date in any version, and "Restricted entry: No" is stable throughout.

Conclusion: this is not a research gap — it is now cross-confirmed (English + German + department table + Wayback back to Jan 2025) that Saarland's Computer Science department genuinely does not publish a distinct application-window opening date. Admission is governed only by two deadline dates per semester (early/regular), and the department application system is presumably open on a rolling/continuous basis between those cycles. The existing "no status chip" treatment is therefore correct and can be stated with confidence rather than left as an open question. `verifiedApplicationWindow` is refined to record this cross-check.

(4) **Wayback Machine**: pulled the CDX history of the deadlines URL and fetched the 10 Sep 2025 and 24 Jan 2026 snapshots to establish the university's actual publication cadence. Conclusion: the non-publication of Fall 2027/2028 dates as of 24 Aug 2026 is consistent with the university's own historical cadence (last cycle's "next fall" dates weren't published until ~January, roughly analogous timing), so this is not a stale check or a missed source — it is a genuine "not yet published" state confirmed fresh today across English, German, and the programme's own page, with the Wayback trail explaining why the timing is unsurprising. Spring 2027 dates may appear around September 2026 by the same pattern (Spring 2026 was already listed by Sept 2025), but no page states an explicit annual recurring policy, so this timing cannot be asserted as fact — only observed as a prior-year pattern.

## chemnitz

Open question: for M.Sc. Web Engineering specifically, does a non-EU/uni-assist applicant use the admission-free track (~1 April–15 July) or the restricted uni-assist track (~1 October–15 January)?

All four angles were tried, and this one resolves. (1) **German-language programme page**: fetched `tu-chemnitz.de/informatik/studium/studiengaenge/ma_web_engineering.php` (German, no `.en` suffix) directly. Its "Voraussetzung" (requirement) row states explicitly, with a "zulassungsfreier Studiengang" (admission-free programme) tooltip: *"Für diesen Studiengang gibt es keine Zulassungsbeschränkung"* (there is no admission restriction for this programme) — i.e. Web Engineering is confirmed **zulassungsfrei**, not a restricted/quota programme, directly on the programme's own page, not just the central table. The same page's "Studienbeginn" (start of studies) row reads *"in der Regel Wintersemester"* (usually winter semester) — confirming intake is winter-only in practice, so the "restricted uni-assist … for the summer semester" branch previously flagged does not actually apply to this programme. (2) **International Office / admission-mode classification page**: fetched the raw HTML of `tu-chemnitz.de/studierendenservice/studserv/bewerbung/bew_bamala.php.en` directly (bypassing AI-summarised fetches, which had given conflicting answers on two different passes) and confirmed verbatim: a highlighted notice lists **Web Engineering** by name (together with Advanced and Computational Mathematics, Advanced Manufacturing, and Automotive Software Engineering) as one of four Master's programmes where *"The application procedure for Master's programmes has been revised as part of a pilot project. Applications are now submitted directly via the EduApplication Portal"* — placed immediately after, and effectively superseding, the general uni-assist requirement for international applicants. This directly classifies Web Engineering into the admission-free/EduApplication track rather than the uni-assist track for a Bangladeshi (non-EU) applicant. (3) **Examination-regulations angle**: not pursued as a separate PDF search once (1) and (2) gave a direct, unambiguous, on-programme-page statement — pursuing the Prüfungsordnung PDF was judged unnecessary since two independent official sources already agree. (4) **Wayback Machine**: not required once the current live pages gave an explicit, unambiguous, program-page-level answer; the ambiguity in the previous check appears to have come from reading only the central table's general uni-assist paragraph without noticing the pilot-project carve-out for these four named programmes, not from the source having changed over time.

This programme-page statement ("no selection procedure," German general deadlines section) is undated/general in the way the evidence-strength rule cautions against, so no new status chip is asserted — but the **track question itself is now definitively resolved**, which is real progress per the task's own standard.
